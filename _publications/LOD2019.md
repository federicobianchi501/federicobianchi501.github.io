excerpt: 'Abstract: District Heating (DH) networks are promising technologies for heat distribution in residential and commercial buildings since they enable high efficiency and low emissions. Within the recently proposed paradigm of smart grids, DH networks have acquired intelligent tools able to enhance their efficiency. Among these tools, there are demand forecasting technologies that enable improved planning of heat production and power station maintenance. In this work, we propose a comparative study for heat demand forecasting methods on a real case study based on a dataset provided by an Italian utility company. We trained and tested three kinds of models, namely non-autoregressive, autoregressive and hybrid models, on the available dataset of heat demand and meteorological variables. The best model, in terms of root mean square error of prediction, was selected. It considers the day of the week, the hour of the day, some meteorological variables, past heat loads and social components, such as holidays. Results show that the selected model is able to achieve accurate 48-hour predictions of the heat demand in several conditions (e.g., different days of the week, different times, holidays and workdays). Moreover, an analysis of the parameters of the selected models enabled to identify a few informative variables.'
