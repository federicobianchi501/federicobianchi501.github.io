I am an AI/ML researcher with a Ph.D. in Computer Science, working at the intersection of reinforcement learning, planning, learning under uncertainty, and reliable machine learning.

My research focuses on developing learning and planning methods for problems involving limited data, imperfect models, and uncertainty. My work spans safe policy improvement, offline and model-based reinforcement learning, Monte Carlo Tree Search, multi-agent reinforcement learning, and uncertainty-aware learning.

Alongside methodological research, I have worked on European-funded projects and industrial collaborations involving real-world applications in energy, defense, and the chemical and pharmaceutical domains. My applied research includes predictive modeling, time-series analysis, data-driven process modeling, experimental design support, process monitoring, and anomaly detection.

Safe and reliable AI

Ongoing research — Reliable robotic control

Building on my previous work, my research aims to extend the principles of Safe Policy Improvement to real-world robotic systems, developing scalable and reliable methods for improving existing control policies without compromising their performance. A central challenge is determining when policy modifications can be trusted despite limited data, imperfect models, distribution shifts, and the long-term consequences of individual decisions. I am investigating methods that combine statistically reliable uncertainty estimation, scalable policy improvement, and behavioral representations learned from offline experience. A key objective is to enable the reliable adaptation of pretrained robotic controllers, leveraging properties such as stability, recoverability, and task progress to guide policy improvements. Ultimately, this research seeks to bridge the gap between theoretical Safe Policy Improvement and its practical application to complex, high-dimensional robotic systems.

Doctoral research projects

Doctoral research | 2021–2024

MCTS-SPIBB & SDP-SPIBB – Scalable Single-Agent Safe Policy Improvement

Research focused on overcoming the computational limitations of traditional Safe Policy Improvement methods, which become impractical in large-scale decision-making environments due to the size of their state and action spaces. I developed two complementary approaches that make reliable policy improvement more computationally tractable while retaining the baseline-relative guarantees of the SPIBB framework. MCTS-SPIBB combines Monte Carlo Tree Search with baseline bootstrapping, concentrating computation on states reachable during planning rather than exhaustively exploring the entire environment. SDP-SPIBB instead improves the scalability of policy iteration by restricting dynamic programming computations to relevant state-action regions supported by the available data. Both methods were theoretically analyzed, establishing convergence to the corresponding SPIBB solutions under the stated assumptions, and extensively evaluated on challenging benchmarks to assess scalability, performance improvement and reliability. The results contributed to publications at ICML 2023 and JAIR 2025.

Doctoral research | 2021–2024

FV-MCTS-SPIBB – Scalable Safe Policy Improvement for Multi-Agent Systems

Research extending Safe Policy Improvement to cooperative multi-agent systems, where the exponential growth of joint state and action spaces makes traditional policy improvement computationally intractable. I developed FV-MCTS-SPIBB, a novel framework that exploits the factorization of multi-agent decision problems to enable coordinated policy improvement without explicitly enumerating all possible joint actions. The approach combines Monte Carlo Tree Search with factored value functions and introduces constrained versions of Max-Plus and Variable Elimination to efficiently identify coordinated actions while respecting baseline-relative safety constraints. I also investigated how factored transition models can improve data efficiency by enabling more informative estimates of state-action visitation. Theoretical analysis established convergence properties under factorization assumptions, while experiments demonstrated scalability to environments with state spaces up to 10⁴¹ and joint action spaces up to 10¹⁶, including cooperative multi-agent and UAV coordination scenarios. The results were published at ICML 2024.

Doctoral research | 2021–2024

MCTS-APW2 – Efficient Planning in Continuous Action Spaces

Research addressing the challenges of decision-making in continuous environments, where the potentially infinite number of available actions makes exhaustive exploration impossible and conventional tree-search methods can require substantial computational resources. I developed MCTS-APW2, an extension of Monte Carlo Tree Search with Action Progressive Widening that improves how new actions are explored during planning. Rather than relying on conventional random action sampling, the proposed method progressively concentrates exploration on promising regions of the continuous action space, increasing the efficiency of the search for high-value decisions. The approach was experimentally evaluated on a continuous navigation benchmark requiring high-precision action selection, demonstrating improved planning performance compared with established MCTS methods. This work contributed to research on scalable planning for continuous control and was presented at AIRO@AIxIA 2022.
